Here is the 8-step VDA-MD hotel onboarding flow in plain terms:
Step 1 — Pick your pilot hotel. Choose one property to start with. Confirm the domain owner (Revenue Manager) and Compliance Officer are identified and available. This hotel becomes the governance baseline for every other property in your portfolio.
Step 2 — Choose your first agent. Select one agent — the one making the most decisions without governance today. The Rate Agent is the recommended start because it has the highest revenue risk and decision volume. Every other agent waits until this one is running cleanly.
Step 3 — Review and approve the governance files. Read the three Markdown files the agent will actually operate against at runtime: the SOP.md (what it must, must not, and may do), the AGENTS.md (its identity, scope, and RACI ownership), and the SKILL.md (which API calls it is permitted to make). Edit any clause that doesn't match your policy. The domain owner approves all three before anything runs.
Step 4 — Set your local exceptions. Encode what makes your property unique on top of the global baseline — your specific rate authority thresholds, loyalty tier rules, preferred supplier conditions. Each exception gets an owner name, an approval date, and a mandatory expiry date. The EXCEPTION.md files write themselves from these inputs.
Step 5 — Run the Crawl phase. The agent operates in shadow mode against live data for a minimum of two weeks. It reads its governance files, makes decisions, and logs them — but writes nothing to Apaleo. Every decision lands in the Shadow Review queue. The domain owner validates each one as correct or incorrect. If a decision is rejected, the system asks whether the rule was wrong (edit the file) or the case was special (add an exception). The Crawl phase does not end until accuracy is at or above 95%.
Step 6 — Pass two HITL gates. Two separate humans must approve before live execution begins. Gate 1 is the domain owner confirming the governance files match actual policy. Gate 2 is the Compliance Officer confirming the files meet minimum compliance standards — correct NIST references, sufficient MUST/MUST NOT clauses, ISO 42001 human oversight clauses present. Both approvals are timestamped and logged permanently to the Witness Agent. Both are required. Neither substitutes for the other.
Step 7 — Walk, then Run. With both gates cleared, the agent executes real decisions in Apaleo. Low-risk decisions (clear PASS, within autonomous ceiling) run autonomously. Medium and high-risk decisions route to the HITL approval queue before executing. After four weeks of Walk phase with the approval patterns documented, the domain owner and Senior GM sign off on which decision categories can progress to fully autonomous Run phase. Categories with frequent corrections stay gated.
Step 8 — Roll out to the portfolio. Once the pilot hotel is in Walk phase, every other property seeds its governance files from that validated baseline. Each property then runs its own Crawl phase — independently, one at a time. The Revenue Manager at each property approves the governance files for their hotel. Improvements made to the baseline at the pilot property flow as proposed pull requests to all downstream properties, which each domain owner must approve before the change takes effect.
The whole sequence is designed around one principle: autonomous authority is earned through demonstrated performance, not granted at deployment. Every hotel earns it separately. Every agent earns it separately.
